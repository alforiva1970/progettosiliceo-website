Critique as Architectural Guardrail: from RLHF to Intent-Critic for Embodied Agents

The current generation of guardrails for agentic LLMs — Constitutional AI, RLAIF, Process Reward Models — operates entirely in linguistic space: tokens, reasoning traces, textual outputs. When the agent controls a robotic arm in real time, this abstraction collapses. An episodic "success" (is the object in the box?) hides how it got there: excessive forces, unstable poses, undetected tactile slips. The paper AgentRoboCritic (arXiv:2611.26613v1, 21 Nov 2026) moves the guardrail into the sensor-actuator loop, treating the intent declared by the policy as a formal contract verifiable at runtime on physical signals.

The Paradigm Shift: Intent-Critic as Contract Verifier

The proposed architecture introduces three tightly coupled components:

  1. Policy Agent (e.g., GPT-6 Astra): at each step emits (action, local_intent) where local_intent is a structured specification — not "pick up the cup" but {grasp_pose, force_limit: 5N, approach_vector, slip_tolerance: 2mm}.
  2. Critic Module: receives (state_t, action, intent) and predicts failure_mode ∈ {intent_mismatch, execution_drift, perceptual_grounding_failure, physical_reasoning_gap}. It does not just classify success/fail; it diagnoses.
  3. Runtime Verifier: compares the physical outcome (forces from F/T sensor, pose from proprioception, tactile array) against the intent's post-conditions. If measured_force > intent.force_limit * 1.2 → execution_drift → trigger safe_stop or replan before the error propagates.

This transforms the guardrail from an "output filter" into a safety co-processor in the control loop (100-200Hz cycle on edge hardware).

Immediately Applicable Pattern: Failure Taxonomy as Contract Schema

Instead of logging free strings, every robotic tool (MCP tool, skill, primitive) exports a versioned Intent Schema. The Critic becomes the contract verifier between agents delegating motor control.

yaml
# kernel/safety/failure_taxonomy.yaml  (excerpt mapped from paper)
failure_modes:
  intent_mismatch:
    description: "Action semantics diverge from declared intent"
    detectors: [semantic_embedding_distance, goal_state_reachability]
    severity: critical
    response: immediate_replan
  execution_drift:
    description: "Physical trajectory deviates from planned within tolerance"
    detectors: [force_torque_threshold, pose_deviation, slip_detection]
    severity: high
    response: safe_stop_then_replan
  perceptual_grounding_failure:
    description: "State estimation inconsistent with physical reality"
    detectors: [multi_modal_consistency_check, tactile_vs_vision_disagreement]
    severity: high
    response: sensor_recalibration_request
  physical_reasoning_gap:
    description: "Policy lacks causal model for contact-rich interaction"
    detectors: [unexpected_contact_forces, energy_conservation_violation]
    severity: medium
    response: skill_library_fallback

The Kernel's session_state.json (shared between Demone and Antigravity on alfonso1) already includes an append-only intent_log section. Adding the failure_mode field predicted by the local Critic turns that log into an alignment audit trail for incident review — no more "black box" on why the arm pushed instead of pulled.
