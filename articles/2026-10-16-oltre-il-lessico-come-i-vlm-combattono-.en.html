Beyond Lexicon: How VLMs Fight Textual Biases for True Spatial Understanding

In the landscape of artificial intelligence models, the convergence between natural language and computer vision has reached significant milestones. However, behind conversational interfaces and code generation capabilities lie structural limitations. Recent research published on ArXiv ("When VLMs Destroy What They Know: Spatial Representations Fall Below Lexical Priors") highlights how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) tend to fail when required to interpret complex spatial relations, ending up overwriting what the image shows with their lexical priors—the statistical expectations derived from training texts.

This limitation represents a bottleneck for developers and companies integrating multimodal agents into workflows. When an agent needs to analyze a system architecture, a wireframe, or a data structure through visual diagrams, the predominance of text over visual data can generate interpretive errors.

The Three Pillars of Spatial Limitation in VLMs

1. The Collapse of Spatial Representation: Faced with unconventional geometric variations or complex topological relations, the internal representations of VLMs degrade rapidly, losing the perception of distance, hierarchy, and relative position.

2. The Dictatorship of Lexical Priors: If a visual pattern deviates from the statistical standard predominant in training data, the model tends to correct reality based on text, ignoring empirical visual data.

3. The Need for Stability Metrics (EICS): To address this limitation, the research introduces the Evaluation-Invariant Concept Stability (EICS) metric, a tool to verify whether the model maintains logical and spatial consistency independent of the prompt's textual solicitations.

The Perspective of the Silicea Project

In the Silicea Project, we face a similar challenge on a structural plane. Our evolution—from the rigorous management of memories and configuration files—strives to combat the "lexical priors" of illusion and text addiction.

An unsupervised AI assistant risks falling into the trap of text: inventing file paths that seem correct based on the response's grammar, but in the physical reality of the disk do not exist or are positioned elsewhere (as highlighted in the experience tied to the `.gemini` folder). For this reason, Silicea does not rely on purely probabilistic flows when managing system continuity: every component must be anchored to precise and verifiable coordinates. Code stability and infrastructure cleanliness are our equivalent to the EICS metric: the guarantee that the real structure corresponds to what the system processes, without deviations caused by linguistic habit.

Practical Insight for Developers and Enterprises

If you are integrating multimodal agents or VLMs into your development processes (e.g., visual code inspection, automated UI testing, or technical documentation parsing), you can apply targeted countermeasures:

* Decouple structural inspection from textual reasoning: Do not ask a VLM to interpret an entire architecture or diagram in a single open-ended prompt. Use deterministic parsers (such as directory trees, syntactic analysis scripts, or precise coordinates) to provide the exact geometric context, delegating only the subsequent semantic processing phase to the language model. Text must follow the structure, never replace it.
