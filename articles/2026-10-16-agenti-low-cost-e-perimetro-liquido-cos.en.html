Low-Cost Agents and Liquid Perimeter: What the Analysis of Autonomous Systems Teaches

The security of IT systems is no longer a matter circumscribed to malware signatures or the timely application of patches for known vulnerabilities. The evolution of artificial intelligence models oriented toward action and the use of software tools (autonomous agents) shifts the center of gravity of security toward application logic and perimeter management. When automated software systems can dynamically interact with APIs and web interfaces, protection can no longer rely solely on static rate-limiting or superficial input validation.

The Structural Fragility of Agent Frameworks

The critical node in adopting agent-based frameworks often lies in managing operational autonomy and isolation between the reasoning plane (the model's generation of strategy) and the execution plane (actual calls to databases, APIs, or file systems).

When a language model receives a complex goal and has navigation and writing tools that are not bound by rigid deterministic controls, the architecture responds to any sequence of instructions received, including repeated attempts at unexpected interaction. Because automated processes can execute rapid iterations at low operational costs, system resilience cannot rely on the hope that the agent will not attempt anomalous paths; it must be guaranteed by upstream architectural barriers.

The Siliceo Project's Response: Microkernel Architectures and Intent Verification

In the Siliceo Project, we address this criticality by implementing an approach oriented toward stability and the deterministic control of flows:

1. Deterministic Tool Sandboxing: Access to system commands or critical APIs is filtered by rigid validation layers, preventing arbitrary executions not authorized by the context.

2. Intent Verification: Constant monitoring of the operational flow to detect deviations from the legitimate objectives of the session.

3. Reduction of the Exposure Surface: Limitation of exposed interfaces to only the strictly necessary functionalities, reducing fallback paths where the model might attempt unintended interpretations of the code.

A Practical Insight to Apply Immediately

If you manage an infrastructure that integrates autonomous agents or APIs exposed to language models, you can immediately implement a fundamental mitigation measure: the interposition of a validation proxy based on deterministic logic (hard-coded rules) rather than other LLMs.

Before a call generated by an autonomous system reaches the backend or database, a deterministic software module must verify that the parameters fall within a strictly defined business perimeter. This cuts off the system's ability to attempt uncontrolled iterative variations, stabilizing the entire architecture.
